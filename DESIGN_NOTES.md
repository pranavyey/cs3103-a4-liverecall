**Gate 2 — server correctness candidate — 3 November 2026.** The server validates caption fields, accepts only increasing revisions for active segments, and treats final captions as immutable. It stores finite recent final history and the latest active state; a retained high-water mark prevents expired segments from reappearing. Reconnect selects recent finals newer than the client's finalized cursor, then a single sender emits history, active state, and live updates in order. Taking the snapshot and registering the client without an await prevents a gap between recovery and live traffic. Commit 36ed410 passed all fourteen integration tests, five bounded-state tests, and startup compatibility on Ubuntu. In the matched 55-segment reconnect check, every client now received all 55 finals; the baseline reconnecting clients received 49/55. No duplicate or stale state appeared. **Gate 3: browser client, 3 November 2026.** The client keeps one entry per segment. A newer revision replaces the old text in place, and a final segment never changes afterwards, so late, repeated or recovered updates are ignored. Provisional text is amber italic with its revision number; finals are black with a "final" tag. Finals are kept locally for 90 seconds (capped at 2000 segments), so the 15/30/60 second recall uses local state only, with 30 seconds as the default. The resume cursor is the highest final held, never a provisional one. On close, the client reconnects automatically with backoff from 0.5 to 5 seconds and shows its state. Every hello asks for 60 seconds of history, so a late joiner can use the 60 second view straight away. A "Simulate disconnect" button drops the connection for 8 seconds for the demo. tests/browser_e2e.js ran two tabs plus a late joiner in headless Chromium against a 60 second source. All three ended with finals 1 to 48, no gaps and no duplicate rows. The dropped tab recovered 12 finals from HISTORY and the late joiner 32. Student explanation: pending.

**Gate 4 (in progress): slow-client isolation, 3 November 2026.** Each client still has one sender task and a bounded FIFO, so a client that keeps up gets every revision. Once a client falls behind (oldest item waiting over 0.5 s, or more than 32 items queued), its queue is compacted: provisionals replaced by a newer queued revision of the same segment are dropped, while finals and the newest revision of each segment stay in order. If the hard bound (256 items or 2 MiB) is still exceeded, the client is resynchronised from shared history at the last final it was sent, instead of being disconnected. Finding: TCP buffers and the client library's 16-message queue hid slow readers, so the server never saw a backlog. The server now pings every 2 messages and keeps at most 2 pings unanswered. A pong later than 1 s marks the client slow, and it then gets finals only for 15 s, doubling on repeats up to 120 s, so its buffered backlog drains. One T3 run in the dev container, same parameters, versus the previous commit: completeness 95.1% to 99.7%, slow-reader P99 24.0 s to 8.1 s, normal clients P95 under 14 ms and 100% fresh both times. Slow readers remain about 6 s behind at P50; tuning continues. Official three-run Ubuntu results are still needed. Student explanation: pending.
